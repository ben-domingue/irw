Tagging

Item 2 closed on 4 September. The gap reopened on the 8th — not through anyone's fault. The documented corpus grew from 4,134 tables to 4,238, the arrivals landed untagged, and every column slipped. Any tag row now reads 98.5%, and the four judged columns that had climbed into the nineties read 88.9% to 97.6%. This is the dynamic #1702 named — the gap widens by construction — arriving four days after the item that closed it.

What moved

Two more batches — 273 tables and then the last 284 — closed out the population this dimension was defined against: 1,427 reachable untagged tables down to 11 on 4 September, across seven batches and 1,580 staged tables in total. Nine of those 11 were sentinels with no reachable source or no dictionary entry. Two further passes landed behind them: a backfill of 369 rows that carried a language and nothing else, and an ISO 639-2 respelling of roughly 550 rows. Through all seven batches construct type never moved once — every agent wrote it and every assembly blanked it, because it measures 50–67% per-atom precision against a 90% bar. That is the gate working, visibly, on the one column that most wanted to be waved through.

The tension

Yesterday this page could say the tag gap was closed. It was — against the population it was defined against. Today status.json counts 62 tables with no tag row, and 58 of them sit in shards 5 and 6, the two newest and the two that grew. Every other shard is at or above 99.8%. Nothing regressed and nothing was withdrawn: new tables arrived, and they arrived untagged. The closure was real, and it answered the question it was asked. The question it did not answer is whether tagging is a project that finishes or a rate that has to keep pace with intake — and 62 tables in four days is the first measurement of that rate. Only the reliability finding is untouched by any of this: 91–94% from two independent re-tag runs, on 60 tables and then 369, is a property of the method rather than of the corpus. So is the part that was never labour — construct type and construct name are definitional, at 54.4% and 54.6%, and no batch will move them.

02

Item text

Coverage is 24.8%, and for the first time since 2 September that is a live count rather than a carried-forward one. The read found two things. Item text has reached 1,052 tables — 297 more than the repo's own figure of 755, which was the best available yesterday. And it no longer lives in one Redivis dataset: it passed the 1000-table cap on 5 September and was split into irw_text and irw_text_2.

What moved

  • Item text was sharded on 5 September. New tables only ever go to the newest dataset, so anything counting irw_text alone is 337 tables short today and drifts further with every batch — and it fails silently, by returning a smaller number rather than an error. The authoritative list is IRW_TEXT_DATASETS in redivis_config.R; both packages, the uploader and the daily drift report read it, and this page's render contract now does too.
  • 476 tables gained item text in eight days — more than the whole corpus carried on 2 September, when this page last had a live read.
  • 367 tables now declare an administered language, against 81 a week ago, and the split shows that is not mostly backfill: 59% of the tables in irw_text_2 carry the column against 23% of the older ones, so new uploads are shipping the schema natively rather than being repaired later.
  • 60 tables were shipping English this project generated with no public note. Every one now carries a line on the issues page saying so. They were invisible because the reconciler globbed only the batch directories.
  • Four published tables were serving each item twice with different text. Three are fixed and live; the fourth sits in a draft awaiting release.

The tension

This section has now been wrong in both directions in three days, and neither time because a number was miscounted. On the 9th it adopted the repo's 755 over a Redivis figure of 579 that was a week stale — correct under the reconciliation rule, and still 297 short. Today's live read says 1,052. The instrument, not the corpus, is what keeps moving. Underneath that, item text is still outrunning tagging: 187 commits to itemtext/ in the last seven days against 43 to tags/, about four and a third to one. The sharding is the sharper lesson. A count that reads one dataset does not break; it returns a smaller number, and a smaller number here reads as item text having lost tables overnight. The two scheduled jobs that touch Redivis daily were already safe, because they enumerate the registry rather than name a dataset. This page was the one that named one.

Still open: 181 tier-B tables need their administered language confirmed, 17 are staged but ungated, and two tables need a decision rather than labour — a scale direction and an instrument naming. Three item-text tables match no row in metadata.csv even case-insensitively, which is either three renamed tables or three orphans; nobody has looked.

03

The Year 3 plan

Three of the first seven items are finished end to end, a fourth was retired rather than completed, and the piece rescued from that retirement — item 6b, the dictionary write path — closed on 6 September. Item 5 broke ground on the 7th, so nothing in the first seven is sitting still. Nothing on the list has moved for two days; what moved is underneath it, in the corpus item 2 was closed against.

The one to look at

The pull-request backlog is not a roadmap item — it is the repo's review habit, and this morning it gave back both mornings of progress: ten pull requests are open, after eight yesterday and ten the morning before. Read the reason before crediting either direction. Not one of the ten has a review — that figure has never moved, at seven of seven, nine of nine, twelve of twelve, ten of ten, eight of eight and now ten of ten. The oldest, #1671, has been open 22 days. What actually changed is the merge side: 5 merged on 9 September, against 26 on the 8th and 43 on the 7th. The queue shrank on the 8th because merging was fast, and grew back on the 9th because merging nearly stopped — never because anyone started reviewing. Every item that moved on this list moved because somebody built a gate. This still asks for a habit instead, and retiring item 6 did not make it someone's job.

The loose end from the retirement is tied off: #1708, the issue still titled [6], was closed on 5 September, so the board, the issue and the plan now agree.
