01

Tagging

Coverage looks like 75% and is really about 55%. The headline number is carried by one derived column, and reading it as progress is the specific mistake the per-column reporting was built to stop.

Hatched bar is computed from each table's own cov_age rather than judged by anyone. It added 775 tables that have a tag row and nothing else in it.

What moved

The real result this cycle was not coverage, it was definition. Writing down what sample's values mean took the auto-tagger's frame facet from 26.9% to 54.5% exact match, and setting from 75.0% to 87.5% — with no change to the tagger at all. Two rounds of stating what a value means bought more than any model or prompt change on offer.

The tension

Seven of the eight columns have barely moved off 55%, and hand tagging cannot track a denominator that grows by ~500 tables a fortnight. The derived column grows with the corpus by construction; nothing else does. Whether any of the other seven has something derivable in it is still unexplored, and that question is worth more than another tagging push.

02

Item text

Coverage is 14% and barely moving. What changed this cycle is that the 14% became honest — the corpus now says which English it wrote itself, and four tables that were quietly serving every item twice were found and three of them fixed.

What moved

  • The administered-language schema shipped: text fields now hold what respondents actually read, with English in parallel _translated columns.
  • 81 tables backfilled — 78 in bulk, plus three where the wording was recovered from the paper for tables the audit could not classify.
  • 60 tables were shipping English this project generated with no public note. Every one now carries a line on the issues page saying so. They were invisible because the reconciler globbed only the batch directories.
  • Four published tables were serving each item twice with different text. Three are fixed and live; the fourth sits in a draft awaiting release.

The tension

This is where the effort is going — 55 commits to itemtext/ in the last seven days against 26 to tags/ — and coverage still moved from 13.5% to 14.0%. That is the honest trade: this cycle bought trustworthiness in the tables that exist rather than more tables. Worth doing once. Worth watching if it repeats.

Still open: 181 tier-B tables need their administered language confirmed, 17 are staged but ungated, and two tables need a decision rather than labour — a scale direction and an instrument naming.

The one to look at

Item 6 is the only untouched item with a measurable cost. Ten pull requests are open, five have no review at all, and one was approved in conversation on 27 July and never merged. With CI now running on every PR, the mechanical half of that review is answered automatically for the first time — which makes this the cheapest it will ever be to clear.
