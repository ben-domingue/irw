Dear Dr. Eitze,

I run the Item Response Warehouse (itemresponsewarehouse.org), a non-commercial archive of item response data at Stanford. It collects datasets of individual item responses, reformats each into one common long format -- one row per person per item -- and republishes them with a citation and a link back to the original deposit, so that methodological work can run across many datasets at once. The project is described in Behavior Research Methods, open access at https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-025-02796-y.

I would like to include the data behind "Using protection motivation theory to explain intentions to vaccinate against tick-borne encephalitis" (Eitze, Maslov & Otten). The OSF project the article points to is public, but it has no licence set, so I am writing to ask rather than to assume.

The simplest thing, if you are willing, is to set a licence on the OSF project (CC0 or CC BY 4.0 are the usual choices). That settles the question for everyone who reads the article, not just for me, and you need not reply -- I will see it and work from it.

Failing that, a reply granting the IRW permission to reformat the dataset and publish the result, attributed to you with a citation and a link to the article, is plenty. I would release it under CC BY 4.0, or under more restrictive terms, or not licensed for onward reuse at all, if you would prefer any of those -- please just say which.

If you would rather we didn't include it, that is an entirely satisfactory answer, and I will record it so that nobody working on the IRW asks you again.
