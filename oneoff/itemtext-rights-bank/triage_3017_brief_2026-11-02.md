## The rules (Ben's standing rulings; apply, do not re-derive)
- The test on any clause: does it RESERVE A RIGHT (fee, permission-required,
  no-derivatives, no-redistribution, "contact the author for other uses", bare
  "non-commercial use" that says nothing about redistribution) → BLOCK, quote it.
  Does it only DISCLAIM FITNESS ("research instrument, not for clinical use") → does not
  block, but quote it in notes.
- Silence on a NAMED third-party instrument → HOLD (2026-10-15, counsel). Name what would
  resolve it (whom to ask / which page to read).
- Depositor-originated wording (study-authored items, the study's own adaptation that
  rewrites the items, an owner's own questionnaire) → SHIP on the deposit licence. A study's
  English rendering of a third-party instrument is still that instrument, not study-authored.
- A word list is not wording (2026-10-17): bare adjectives, category labels, name lists with
  no stems and no instructions → SHIP. Full declarative sentences are wording.
- A positive public-domain / no-cost / "free to use and reproduce" statement from the
  originator → SHIP, quote it. A grant scoped to USE ("may be used for academic research")
  that says nothing about redistribution → BLOCK (2026-10-18, ERQ). An author letter with a
  courtesy request attached is not permission (2026-09-10).
- CC BY-NC / BY-NC-SA licence, or an originator statement that permits non-commercial
  reproduction AND distribution → SHIP_WITH_NOTE (licence carried on the table).
- The originator's terms beat a downstream CC BY paper that reprints the scale (2026-09-08).
  Exception: a reprint BY THE ORIGINATORS under an OPEN licence (CC BY article, CC0 deposit)
  → SHIP. A paywalled originator article is not a licence to inherit (SIMS, 2026-10-18).
- A translation or adaptation is a derivative of the restricted instrument; a block or hold
  on the original reaches it. A public-domain statement beside an NC clause: the restriction
  governs.
- Government / public-survey items (census, GSS, PISA-style public questionnaires, WHO
  instruments) still need their own terms read; do not assume public domain.
