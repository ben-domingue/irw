```yaml
- table: de_vries_2022_bat_burnout_core
  issue: |-
    The Dutch wording shipped here is the official work-related Burnout Assessment Tool questionnaire, not text published by this study: the study's own PLOS deposit carries no variable or value labels for any BAT column and the paper prints only two example items. The item codes are the deposit's own column names and their prefix names the subscale, so subscale membership is exact — each subscale's item row-means reproduce the deposit's own BAT scores — but the position of an item within its 5- or 8-item block rests on the order the official questionnaire presents them in, and nothing in the data distinguishes one within-block position from another.
- table: de_vries_2022_bat_secondary
  issue: |-
    As with the core BAT table, the wording is the official Dutch BAT questionnaire's rather than the study's: neither the deposit (no labels on any BAT column) nor the paper, which describes only the 23 core items, publishes the secondary-symptom wording. The instructions and the 1-5 anchors are likewise the canonical form's — the scale was administered inside a larger online survey whose framing text was never published — and the assignment of batspan01-05/batsoma01-05 to positions 1-5 within each block follows the official form's order.
- table: deilkas_2019_patient_safety_climate
  issue: |-
    This Safety Attitudes Questionnaire was administered in Norwegian, but no Norwegian wording appears anywhere in the study's deposit or supplements, so the item and option text shipped here is the study's own English, taken from the SPSS variable and value labels of its CC BY 4.0 PLOS deposit — not the wording respondents read. Separately, Q34 and Q59 ship byte-identical `item_text`: their source variable labels differ only by the questionnaire numbering prefix they carry ('31.' vs '56.'), which is stripped on the way in. Q59/Q60 are the instrument's usual emergency/routine fatigue pair, so it is Q34's label that is most likely a copy-paste error in the deposit; it is shipped as labelled rather than blanked, and its item text should be treated as uncertain.
- table: dejesus_2017_lequesne
  issue: |-
    The study deposited both the Portuguese questionnaire it administered and its own English rendering, and the two disagree at three points: the everyday-life squat item reads 'Para agachar-se completamente' in Portuguese and 'Can you pick up an object from the floor?' in English, the morning-stiffness anchors differ, and the walking-distance anchor for level 1 reads 'superior a 1 hora' against '> 1 kilometer but limited'. Both are shipped as written, with the administered Portuguese in `item_text` and the study's English in `item_text_translated`. The maximum-distance item is scored 0-6 by distance band with 1 or 2 added for walking aids, so its levels 7 and 8 are composites with no published label and ship blank.
- table: dejesus_2017_sf36
  issue: |-
    The Brazilian-Portuguese questionnaire this study administered prints 1 = Sim / 2 = Nao for the yes/no items of questions 4 and 5, but the deposited data stores them as 0/1, so the option codes here follow the data rather than the printed form. That direction was established from the data, not assumed: for all seven yes/no items the resp=1 group has the worse physical-functioning block mean (1.49-1.61 against 2.16-2.29). The table also holds only 22 of the SF-36's 36 items — questions 1-8. Questions 9-11 were administered, since the paper reports domain scores that need them, but they are absent from the study's deposited data file.
- table: derubeis_2017_arsq
  issue: |-
    The wording respondents read is the German in `item_text`, taken verbatim from the deposited SPSS file's own variable labels; **the English in the `_translated` columns was generated by this project, not by the study**, and has not been checked against a published German-to-English ARSQ. Beyond that, the ARSQ presents 20 hypothetical situations, each rated on two sub-items (A = how anxious you would be, B = how likely the other person is to respond positively), and the situation vignettes themselves appear neither in the study's deposit nor in the paper — so `section_prompt` is blank and only the two sub-item questions are given. Scale points 2-5 are unlabelled in the source and ship blank.
- table: dinic_2025_shortdarktriad
  issue: |-
    This table pools 14 samples administered in 12 languages, so the item text shown is the English SD3 master wording rather than the strings most respondents read, and no single administered language could be named. At items sd3_2, sd3_7, sd3_8 and sd3_26 the study's own two sources disagree: the paper's Table 3 prints the canonical SD3 wording, which is what ships, while the deposited SPSS file's variable label at those four positions quotes a statement that is not an SD3 item at all (for sd3_26, 'I like to pick on losers.'). The other 23 are identical in both. Responses for sd3_11, sd3_15, sd3_17, sd3_20 and sd3_25 are stored already reverse-recoded, so for those five resp 1 means 'Agree strongly' and resp 5 'Disagree strongly' — the opposite of the other 22.
- table: direkvand_2022_mjsi
  issue: |-
    This instrument was administered in Persian and the Persian version is deposited, but it could not be attached to the item codes: it numbers its items 1-25 continuously and groups the professional and physical-mental items differently from the paper's Table 4, which is the numbering the codes follow, and three items differ outright between the two. So the study's own English version ships instead of the wording respondents read. No response-option text is shipped at all: the published instrument prints one uniform 5-point scale for every item, while the deposited SPSS file's value labels give most items a 2-, 3- or 4-category scale that matches the response ranges actually observed — the two accounts contradict each other and neither could be established, so resp 1-5 ships unlabelled. Item-to-code matching is verified at the level of the five dimensions and not for the order of items within a dimension; note also that only two of the paper's six published Cronbach alphas reproduce from the deposited data.
```
